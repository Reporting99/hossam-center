Modern Honda cars come equipped with **Honda Sensing**, an advanced suite of safety and driver-assistive technologies designed to keep you and your passengers safe. This system relies on two critical components: a radar sensor mounted in the front grille/bumper and a camera mounted behind the windshield. 

However, for these safety features to work accurately, precise calibration of these sensors is vital. In this article, we explain what ADAS calibration is and when your Honda requires it.

## 1. What is ADAS / Radar Calibration?

Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS) like Honda Sensing require perfect alignment. A deviation of even one millimeter at the sensor level can translate to several meters of error on the road, causing systems to behave erratically or fail during emergency situations.

Calibration involves using specialized targeting boards and diagnostic computer scanners to adjust the electronic angles of the camera and radar, aligning them back to the manufacturer’s exact specifications.

## 2. When Does Your Honda Require Sensing Calibration?

Your vehicle’s safety sensors must be re-calibrated in any of the following scenarios:
*   **Windshield Replacement**: Since the front camera is attached directly to the windshield, replacing the glass changes the camera's angle of view.
*   **Front-End Collision or Bumper Repair**: Even a minor bump to the front bumper can dislodge or misalign the radar sensor.
*   **Suspension Changes or Wheel Alignment**: Altering the ride height of the vehicle changes the pitch and level of the camera and radar.
*   **Warning Lights on the Dashboard**: If your dashboard displays errors like "Radar Obstruction", "Camera Malfunction", or "Sensing System Stopped", active calibration is required.

## 3. The Dangers of Ignoring Calibration

Neglecting radar and camera calibration can result in serious safety hazards:
*   **Unexpected Emergency Braking**: The vehicle may misinterpret innocent road objects as obstacles, triggering automatic braking.
*   **Lane Keep Assist Failures**: The car may drift out of lanes or fail to recognize lane lines.
*   **Adaptive Cruise Control Discrepancies**: The vehicle may tail other cars too closely.
